What Warsh actually did
Killing Forward Guidance Is Not a Neutral Act
Forward guidance was the Fed's most powerful tool since 2008 — the ability to move markets without touching rates. By removing it, Warsh transferred uncertainty back to the market. This is deliberate. His academic work has argued for years that guidance creates moral hazard and distorts risk pricing.

The implication: every asset class must now be priced on fundamentals alone, without the Fed backstop anchor. That's structurally bearish for high-multiple growth assets (AI stocks, speculative tech) and mixed for emerging markets — higher uncertainty premium, but also higher expected returns for those who navigate it correctly.

Bull case
Why This Could Be Positive
A Fed that doesn't pre-commit can cut faster when data deteriorates. If CRE defaults accelerate or unemployment spikes, Warsh can pivot aggressively without losing credibility — because he never promised anything. Optionality is a two-way street.
Bear case
Why This Is Dangerous
Markets were priced for 2 cuts in H2 2026. That anchor is now gone. With 9/18 members seeing a hike and $875B in CRE debt maturing this year, the probability distribution of outcomes just got much wider in both directions.
